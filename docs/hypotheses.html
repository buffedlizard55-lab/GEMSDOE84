Rank 1 · highest research priority · moderate cost

Cross-physics edge persistence with a matched-null penalty

Layers: reduced-to-pole / total magnetic intensity and its vertical/horizontal slopes; isostatic gravity anomaly and slope; dilatation, shear strain rate, and second strain invariant. All are named in the official feature description.

Signature: derive multiscale directional edge ridges from the existing continuous fields, then retain narrow alignments whose position and orientation persist across independent physical channels and scales. Score co-located edge persistence, not a union of every high-gradient pixel. The null is a spatially shifted or orientation-matched set of edges within the same broad terrain/coverage stratum.

Why it might find an uncatalogued fault: a buried or poorly exposed structure can create a coherent contrast in more than one geophysical field without coinciding with a mapped trace; requiring cross-channel geometric consistency may suppress isolated lithologic contacts and single-layer noise. That is a testable hypothesis, not proof of fault causation.

Main mimic: lithologic boundaries, survey-line leveling seams, broad basin margins, and correlated processing artifacts can also generate persistent edges.

Difference from this repo: no hypothesis/model implementation exists here (only documentation and utility checks), so this is distinct from implemented methods in this checkout; novelty against sibling sites or earlier sessions is unknown. Unlike a raw multi-layer sum/union, the proposed method is persistence-and-null-calibrated edge co-location. This exact distinction must be checked against the recovered experiment ledger.

Holdout plan: test each derived channel alone as a leakage canary; then test the preregistered cross-channel statistic on whole-segment spatial blocks, using visible catalogue-derived features only and pixel-exact masking. Keep emitted mass matched to the incumbent. Report pooled HOLDOUT-DTI with evaluator version, withheld-positive count and 95% CI. No score is available now.

Rank 2 · moderate potential · moderate cost

Scale-space ridge/valley curvature with drainage-conditioned controls

Layers: detrended elevation and its slope. Derive profile/plan curvature at several neighborhood scales; suppress or condition on convergent drainage-like forms rather than treating all linear terrain breaks as faults.

Signature: long, locally coherent curvature transitions that persist across scales and are not explained by a branching drainage network.

Why it may add faults: subtle fault scarps or warped surfaces may remain visible in topographic derivatives even where traces are absent from the catalogue.

Mimics: gullies, road cuts, lithologic benches, landslides, and DEM artifacts. Existing listed projects include many topographic/scarp ideas, so this has substantial duplication risk outside this checkout.

Difference here: no terrain transform is implemented in this repo; the proposed discriminator is explicit drainage conditioning and scale persistence, not a single-scale ridge score. Sibling novelty is unverified.

Rank 3 · moderate potential · moderate-to-high cost

Strain-anomaly corridors gated by independent magnetic edges

Layers: dilatation, shear strain rate, second strain invariant, plus vertical/horizontal TMI slopes.

Signature: identify elongated strain-anomaly corridors whose termination, offset, or boundary aligns with a magnetic edge; downweight broad strain domains lacking a narrow structural boundary.

Why it may add faults: active deformation can make an otherwise unmapped structure visible in a deformation proxy, while a magnetic contact can localize its geometry.

Mimics: interpolation artifacts, regional tectonic gradients, non-fault strain sources, and unrelated lithologic contacts. Explicitly test each feature alone for leakage and spatial autocorrelation.

Difference here: interaction/gating of an existing strain family by a separate magnetic edge, rather than a generic multivariate predictor. No implementation exists in this checkout; related work elsewhere is unknown.

Rank 4 · lower expected lift · high cost

Earthquake-density discontinuity as an independent fault cue

Layer: provided earthquake density, analyzed with nearby strain and topographic context only for interpretation, not as a label-derived predictor.

Signature: persistent narrow gradients or aligned density corridors that intersect a structural edge; avoid emitting directly on the earthquake-density maximum.

Why it may add faults: some active but unmapped faults may organize seismicity along a line distinct from existing mapped traces.

Mimics: swarm processes, geothermal fluid migration, catalog completeness variations, and clustered earthquakes not caused by a single surface fault. The provided density product's temporal window and construction must be audited before use.

Difference here: a physically distinct seismicity-geometry interaction, not a fault-catalogue proximity feature. No implementation exists here; exact prior tests cannot be checked.

Rank 5 · exploratory · high cost / external-data check required

Radiometric alteration edge independent of the base feature stack

Layers/source: GeoDAWN airborne radiometric grids from the USGS GeoDAWN data release, DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ. USGS describes the release as including downloadable geophysical grids and states the release rights as CC0 1.0; verify specific product, coordinate system, coverage, and terms before use.

Signature: cross-validated K/Th/U ratio or ternary-radioelement boundaries spatially concordant with existing-stack structural edges, with flight-height and radon corrections audited against the source metadata.

Why it may add faults: hydrothermal alteration and fluid pathways may change near-surface radioelement signatures independently of topographic expression.

Mimics: soil moisture, regolith/lithology changes, radon variability, and survey altitude/processing seams. Radiometric edge ≠ fault or geothermal system.

Difference here: no radiometric data or external-data ingestion exists in this repo. The official USGS landing page establishes that the source release exists, but actual file download, coverage, license verification at product level, and alignment have not been performed in this environment. This is not currently viable under the cached-stack-only lane.
