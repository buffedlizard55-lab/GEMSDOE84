Rank 1 · highest priority from prior review · moderate cost

Conductive-base / surface-conductivity break coherence

Layers: surface conductivity and depth to conductive base, both named in the official feature stack.

Physical signature: aligned, scale-persistent lateral breaks that cohere between the surface-conductivity field and conductive-base geometry; test an edge-coherence interaction against orientation- and location-matched nulls, not a catalogue-trained classifier.

Why it could identify an uncatalogued fault: a buried fluid pathway or altered structure may perturb conductivity and the geometry of a conductive base where there is no mapped surface trace. The mechanism is conditional and does not equate conductivity with geothermal productivity.

Named non-fault mimic: lithologic/clay boundaries, salinity variation, groundwater, and survey-resolution transitions can generate the same signature.

Difference from this repository: there is no geological detector here; this paired conductivity/depth edge-coherence feature is distinct from the utility-only code currently in the checkout. However, the GEMSDOE83 archive mentions conductivity-breach/alteration coupling, so this may overlap prior sibling work. Novelty is not established until the exact prior run registry and code are compared.

Holdout plan: apply the leakage canary to each channel and the interaction alone; derive catalogue features only from visible faults; withhold whole segments with buffer, mask visible faults pixel-exactly, and use the shared pooled DTI evaluator. Match emitted mass to the incumbent and report evaluator version, withheld-positive count, and 95% CI. No HOLDOUT-DTI value exists here.

Rank 2 · potentially useful · moderate cost

Cross-scale magnetic contact persistence

Layers: reduced-to-pole magnetic anomaly, total magnetic intensity, and provided vertical/horizontal TMI slopes.

Signature: scale-space edge persistence with orientation consistency across magnetic representations, rather than a single-band threshold.

Why it could add faults: buried fault boundaries may juxtapose magnetic units and retain a linear edge even where the surface expression is weak.

Mimics: ordinary lithologic contacts, intrusive bodies, survey-line processing seams.

Difference and caveat: no such code in this repo; magnetic/fusion work is claimed in sibling archives, so exact novelty cannot be inferred from titles alone.

Rank 3 · moderate potential · low-to-moderate cost

Strain-transition corridors

Layers: dilatation rate, shear strain rate, and second invariant of strain rate.

Signature: localized sign/gradient transitions and linearly organized strain concentration, evaluated with spatial blocks to prevent catalogue memorization.

Why it could add faults: deformation discontinuities may expose active structural zones beyond mapped Quaternary traces.

Mimics: geodetic interpolation smoothness, block boundaries, regional deformation, and non-fault strain sources.

Difference and caveat: no strain-transition detector exists here; similar strain/tensor coupling is discussed in the GEMSDOE83 archive, so prior overlap must be resolved.

Rank 4 · moderate potential · moderate-to-high cost

Gravity–magnetic cross-gradient junctions

Layers: isostatic gravity anomaly and slope plus magnetic gradients.

Signature: junctions where independent density and magnetic boundaries align or terminate, scored geometrically rather than by adding anomaly magnitudes.

Why it could add faults: buried structural junctions may organize permeability and remain unmapped at the surface.

Mimics: stratigraphic contacts, inversion artifacts, and unrelated boundaries that coincide by chance.

Difference and caveat: this exact cross-gradient junction score is not implemented here, but both physical families appear in sibling archive descriptions. Novelty is unverified.

Rank 5 · exploratory · low-to-moderate cost

Earthquake-density lineament versus swarm null

Layer: provided earthquake-density feature, interpreted with existing structural context only after its construction and time window are verified.

Signature: elongated density gradients that align with a linear structure; compare against matched clustered/swarm controls so spatial clustering alone cannot drive the result.

Why it could add faults: some active unmapped structures may organize seismicity along a line not represented in mapped fault traces.

Mimics: geothermal-fluid swarms, catalog completeness, and clustered earthquakes without a surface fault.

Difference and caveat: no such interaction is implemented here. Earlier sibling usage and the provided density layer’s provenance/window are unknown; treat as lower-priority until audited.
