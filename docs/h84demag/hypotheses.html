The physical mechanism (H84-1/H84-3), with literature

Hydrothermal fluid circulation along a fault destroys primary magnetite (demagnetization) and deposits clay/argillic alteration minerals that raise near-surface electrical conductivity. This produces a local magnetic-amplitude minimum conjugate with a conductivity high, and the halo can be broad and blind (no mapped surface rupture) — exactly the kind of structure missing from the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue that the competition is scoring for. This is documented, peer-reviewed geophysics, not a guess:

  • Bouligand et al. 2014, JGR Solid Earth, "Distribution of buried hydrothermal alteration deduced from high-resolution magnetic surveys in Yellowstone National Park" — full text
  • Finn & Morgan 2002 / USGS, "High-resolution aeromagnetic mapping of volcanic terrain, Yellowstone National Park" — USGS page
  • 2026, Minerals (MDPI), "Integrated Multiphysics Inversion for Geothermal and Lithium Exploration in Dixie Valley, Nevada" (within the Great Basin, the same structural province as the GeoDAWN survey area) — full text

Why it failed — the mechanism, not just the number

Not leakage (all AUCs ≈0.51, far below the 0.90 threshold). We measured the real cause directly: 8.16% of the entire footprint lies within the 300 m kernel radius of some mapped fault — the catalogue is geographically pervasive across this Basin-and-Range terrain, so any spatially-uniform field earns substantial credit purely from ubiquity. Our field instead concentrates its highest-confidence mass in specific conductive sub-basins whose geometry does not coincide with the basin-margin trend the mapped network follows — i.e. the named non-fault mimic we tried to guard against (broad, equant, undeformed basin-fill producing the same joint magnetic-low/conductivity-high signature with zero fault control) dominated the field's extreme tail despite the Sato ridge-filter guard meant to suppress it.

This does not prove the underlying geological mechanism (demagnetized alteration haloes along blind faults) is wrong — it proves this specific field, scored against the public catalogue (itself biased toward easy, well-mapped surface ruptures), does not reproduce that catalogue better than chance. The real competition scores against genuinely new faults, a target this sandbox cannot evaluate directly. See Suggested next steps for how a future session should close this gap.
